# What the caffeine curve can tell someone

Reviewed on 23 November 2026. Kachka knows which entries someone saved, their reported times and how the dose was supplied. A person can use these facts to check a serving, compare modelling assumptions or see which drink contributes to the bedtime figure. The app has no observation of blood concentration, sleep or individual clearance.

## Model decision

An intake-only timeline would have the fewest physiological assumptions and is the appropriate fallback when all doses are unknown. It loses the ability to explain carryover. A fitted absorption/elimination model could describe a specified population and formulation if calibrated, but this log contains neither a concentration series nor enough information to fit an individual. Retain the illustrative curve for entries with usable amounts and expose its assumptions.

For each included entry, convert a per-serving or per-100 mL quantity to consumed mg using volume where required and the fraction consumed. Before consumption its contribution is zero. In the first 50 minutes, multiply the dose by a quarter-sine rise from zero to one. Thereafter multiply it by 0.5 raised to elapsed hours since peak divided by the chosen half-life. Sum all contributions at the same instant. The sine shape, instantaneous recorded serving time, full-dose peak and linear addition are engineering assumptions. Re-running the sum at 2 and 8 hours illustrates sensitivity to decay around the 5-hour example. The band has no statistical coverage or time/dose uncertainty built in.

The 48-hour lookback and 04:00 local day serve different purposes. At 48 hours a dose still has about 1.7% of its model peak under the slow scenario. Earlier doses are omitted for a bounded view. Unknown amounts are omissions even when other entries have labels. Rough times and stale defaults can shift the model in either direction. A label is evidence about the serving, not a measurement of the consumer. A material bedtime contributor is shown only when the five-hour model attributes at least 1 mg to an actual entry. Ties prefer the more recent entry. This threshold controls rounding and legibility only.
