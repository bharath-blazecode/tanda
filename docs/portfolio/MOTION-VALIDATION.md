# Stand Still evidence and phone validation

## What the number measures

The phone is held upright against the chest with both hands, eyes open and feet together. The task lasts ten seconds, including two seconds to settle. In gyroscope mode the input is rotation rate about device x and y (DeviceMotion beta and gamma, degrees/second). In linear acceleration mode the inputs are device x and y (metres/second squared). Gravity-inclusive acceleration helps assess placement. The RMS is the square root of mean squared filtered values. The combined RMS is the hypotenuse of the two axis RMS values. The numbers describe movement of the phone, including hand movement.

Device axes rotate with the phone. In portrait against the chest, y runs approximately up its long edge. Motion in or around that axis is not a calibrated anatomical side-to-side displacement. A force plate measures another quantity again. Comparing across sources, mounting conventions or body positions confounds the result. Browser time stamps characterize delivered events. They do not establish sensor hardware latency. [W3C Device Orientation and Motion](https://www.w3.org/TR/orientation-event/)

The nominal 0.2–3 Hz filter attenuates offsets and fast change. Its response depends on sampling and filter warm-up. Eight seconds contains only 1.6 cycles at 0.2 Hz. Changing duration, sample spacing or discarding a different portion can change RMS even for genuine movement. Near-zero floors distinguish some stationary fixtures from usable signals. They do not identify who is standing, a clinically meaningful amount of movement, or the cause of a change. The engine's provisional 30% sway floor is a product setting pending unchanged-retest data.

The repository evidence ledger records Caccese et al.'s chest-held balance task as the closest match in duration and placement. Stance-specific repeatability of a study score does not transfer automatically to these axes, filters, hardware or outcome. It also records Jongen et al.'s longer force-plate trials, which provide group-level context for a different instrument and endpoint. The full text of the former returned HTTP 403 in this session. These conclusions use the existing review. No physical or clinical agreement was established by B2. ## Engineering pilot on physical phones

1. Use an HTTPS instance of the exact review SHA. Test at least one iPhone with Safari and one Android phone with Chrome, in their system browsers. Use anonymized trial IDs. Record hardware, OS, browser, build, source and units, screen orientation, footwear, floor, lighting, sound mode and permission state. Keep the initial grant, denial and no-event trials in the log. A skipped step is a useful outcome.
2. In separate fresh sessions try allowing and denying motion. Test no permission prompt where supported. Record whether Setup availability agrees with the Stand task. Try Cancel during countdown, background or lock during capture, navigate away, then return and retry Stand alone. Repeat each three times per phone. Check the actual sound, silent mode, countdown, end cue, replay and ticket. A blocked task must allow the rest of the check to continue.
3. For repeatability, recruit five consenting adults comfortable with the standing task. Use a level surface, nearby support, a fixed eye-level target, identical foot position and the same chest position for each participant. One practice trial per phone precedes scored trials. Stop if uncomfortable. Run three scored trials per phone per person on each of two days, with at least 30 seconds' rest between trials. Alternate phone order across participants and retain order within the data. This gives 60 primary trials. Avoid substances and deliberate destabilization.
4. Record every attempt. Note time, source, protocol, measured cadence, capture/usable duration, result status, RMS values, whether a retry or recovery occurred and any placement change. In a supervised local validation build or remote browser inspector optionally collect event-time deltas in memory to summarize median, p95 and longest gap. Record unavailable fields as missing. Keep the two days, original attempts and retries distinguishable. Do not infer compatibility from similar RMS values.
5. Include negative controls. Repeat each three times per phone: phone flat and untouched, deliberate reorientation during capture, and a screen lock mid-task. Test same-device/same-protocol comparisons and use controlled test records for legacy, different-unit and different-protocol comparisons if a physical browser cannot select the relevant source. Inspect whether a real comparison was admitted and whether unrelated valid tasks survived a failed Stand. Annotate flat-phone results separately because a vibrating table or sensor noise can produce physical motion.
6. For each participant/device/day, report measured availability out of all primary trials, median RMS, min/max, and the absolute percentage change from the first scored trial for comparable, non-negligible baselines. Report absolute differences in source units when a baseline is too small for a stable percentage. Compare first versus later trials and day-to-day differences. Count unchanged pairs exceeding the current 30% product floor. Keep data stratified by source, device and recovery status. This small study can expose failure modes, not estimate population diagnostic sensitivity.
7. Engineering release gates: zero incompatible scored comparisons, zero active capture after cancellation/navigation, zero background intervals silently counted as continuous capture, and an honest retry or skip for every failed recording. All are correctness invariants. Treat fewer than 90% usable uninterrupted trials on either phone as a usability investigation trigger (more than roughly one retry in ten). If unchanged retests routinely cross the scoring floor, pause claims about individual change and collect a larger calibration set. Preserve failures, timestamps and conditions before altering thresholds.
8. Physical agreement requires a separate experiment. Rigidly align a calibrated reference IMU with the phone, synchronize timing with markers before and after the capture, express signals in the same axes/units and process the identical time window and filter convention. Compare signed and absolute paired errors across repeated stationary and controlled oscillatory trials at several rates/amplitudes. Report bias, spread, clipping and disagreement by device and source. Without this reference, report repeatability and availability only.

## Browser reproduction

Run the production build locally as described in `scripts/browser-checks/README.md`, then run `python scripts/browser-checks/motion.py` with Playwright and Chromium. Set `KACHKA_TEST_ORIGIN` or `CHROMIUM_PATH` if needed. The script checks denied permission, a late permission grant after cancellation, simulated backgrounding, navigation during capture and completed runs with and without Web Audio. It reports measured event cadence and synthetic dispatch gap statistics. UI HOLD-to-replay timing includes Playwright observation latency.

The lo-fi cue creates Web Audio oscillators and schedules their envelopes. It loads or decodes no audio files. A separate headless Chromium timing probe ran six alternating blocks of 120 nominal 60 Hz timer ticks. Cue setup took 1.0–11.7 ms. The cue-on p95 tick gap was 16.1–16.2 ms versus 16.1 ms with the cue off. This desktop probe gave no evidence of a sustained timing penalty. Phone audio latency, vibrate behavior, thermal throttling and mobile power management remain to be measured.
