# Kachka commercial release evidence

Reviewed 24 November 2026 against the Phase F baseline `0dbcbf15c3cf` and Phase G release changes. The reviewed scope is an adult entertainment and self-observation product. A consumer launch needs a named operator, an accurate collection notice, commercial rights to the team assets and review of the claims that people actually see. A team review or passing test suite cannot establish medical validity or legal compliance.

Phase D inspected the curated PK-DB record for Abernethy and Todd (1985). Two groups of nine nonsmoking women took 162 mg caffeine. Group mean time to peak was 1.52/0.79 h and half-life 7.88/5.37 h with/without oral contraceptive use. That evidence demonstrates variation. It does not validate Kachka's quarter-sine rise, an individual's half-life, or the ends of the scenario range. Original full text was unavailable. The EFSA opinion and the Drake sleep study were bibliographic references in the earlier review. No new human observations or full-text scientific review took place here. ## AI controls and monthly operating model

Phase G makes `VERDICT_ENABLED=false` the default. Keep it false, `VERDICT_DAILY_CALL_LIMIT=0` and the key absent. Any one of these conditions closes the paid path. Eligible requests require explicit server enablement, a key and a positive remaining allowance. A previously opted-in client may still send a summary to the Kachka server. Fresh profiles use the local lines by default. The origin receives a device ID as a limiter key. If enabled, the provider gets verdict/confidence, mode, coarse drift bands, rounded caffeine mg, alcohol count, local hour and applicable demo context. The prompt omits names, device IDs, raw signals and free-text drink logs. Logs in the verdict handler contain outcome and reason. Provider, edge, crash and hosting logs have separate operational policies.

The service falls back to the team's canned lines on outage, refusal, throttling, filtered reply or deadline. The browser waits up to 4 s, the server 3 s, with no SDK retries and a 150-output-token cap. The steady-with-alcohol path always uses the fixed safe lines. A timed-out or locally rejected upstream result may still be billed. Fixed-window limits are 12 attempts/device/hour, 2,000/IP/hour, 150/IP/minute, 200 global/minute and 0–6,000 per process per 24 h. Restarts reset counters. Multiple replicas multiply allowances. The application cannot provide a hard provider billing ceiling.

For genuine demand inputs, the visible room polls every 1.5 s and active consenting tabs heartbeat every 15 s. Five always-visible boards over four hours imply about 48,000 scheduled reads. One hundred devices present for two hours imply about 48,000 scheduled heartbeats in addition to joins, changes and retries. Multiply measured response bytes by read count for egress. Browser receipt generation has no image-upload fee. Increasing the hosting allowance by A$20 or moving Redis to a separate assumed A$25 allocation changes the illustrative total to A$62 or A$67, respectively. These are planning assumptions. No invoice, live resource usage or current VPS tariff was verified.

For a future approved paid trial, a single uninterrupted process with 20 attempts/day over 30 aligned windows gives 600 attempts. If each consumes 600 input and 150 output tokens, monthly provider cost in its billing currency is `0.36 × price per million input tokens + 0.09 × price per million output tokens`, before tax, FX and any provider-specific charges. Input length and prices are assumptions until measured and quoted. Confirm model availability and current prices. Select the dedicated Kachka organization/workspace in the provider Console, open billing and limits, capture the hard-cap amount and scope, and distinguish it from a soft alert. Set alerts at 50/80/100% of the approved budget and identify who receives them. Test fallback with a fake client. Leave paid calls disabled pending signed-off budget, external hard cap, data-processing terms and a server-only key.
